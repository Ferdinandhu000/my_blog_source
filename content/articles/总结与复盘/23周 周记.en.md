Today is the second day of the Gaokao. I recalled various things that happened in high school and my own expectations for university back then, and now I realize that many things turned out very differently from what I imagined. In the past, I thought university would be an extremely free academic sanctuary, and some of the subjects I unfortunately had to study were all meant to help me reach greater heights so I could access areas I was truly interested in and willing to explore. However, at present, I find that university has actually become quite similar to high school. This similarity is not reflected in students’ choice of majors, but actually stems from their self-identity awareness. Everyone still seems to be immersed in the student identity of the past 12 years and cannot extricate themselves, treating performance-oriented utilitarianism as the core standard of evaluation. The concept of “GPA is king” has deeply rooted itself, forcing people to develop a strong "self-motivation" to adapt to such a competitive environment, not realizing that this so-called "self-motivation" no longer comes from the "self" but from the "GPA."
Everyone didn’t even have a chance to catch their breath before being asked to firmly decide on their own planning route early on. Such a university life is indeed not what I want, at least it’s not what my past self would have wanted. At the beginning of this semester, when I was choosing a general education course and asked a senior for help, I learned that the course "Appreciation and Design of Ancient Chinese Architecture" was an online general education course, with relatively simple assessments and very easy to get high scores. So I happily enrolled in this course, not realizing that from that moment on, the purpose of choosing courses would no longer be pure; all I saw was the desire for high scores, without any commitment to my interests (haha). It’s only this week that a test is coming up (I hadn’t gotten the question bank yet, but I had watched all the course videos), that I searched online for information about the final exam of this course. By chance, I saw a post about this course, which mentioned that when it comes to the chapter on the protection of ancient architecture, hearing about the demolition of ancient buildings is particularly upsetting. In fact, I also felt deeply moved when I was listening to that lesson. Mr. Liang Sicheng made every effort back then to prevent the demolition of Beijing’s ancient city walls, yet ultimately to no avail; the artistic aesthetics and artistic achievements of many generations were destroyed in an instant (seems like I’m digressing). In fact, I think this is the true meaning of the design of general education courses. However, how much discussion like this about the content of this course exists online? The current situation is that if you search "Chaoxing Learning Pass ‘Appreciation and Design of Ancient Chinese Architecture’", the first things that pop up are either chapter exercise answers or question bank explanations, which is truly lamentable.
